# EASA PPL to FAA Private Pilot Certificate (BASA/TIP‑L)

*A guide to converting your EASA Part‑FCL Private Pilot Licence (PPL) to a U.S. FAA Private Pilot Certificate under the Bilateral Aviation Safety Agreement (BASA) and Technical Implementation Procedures – Licensing (TIP‑L)*

---

## Legal Framework: BASA & TIP‑L

The U.S.–EU Bilateral Aviation Safety Agreement (BASA), through its **Annex 3 – Pilot Licensing Annex** and the **Technical Implementation Procedures – Licensing (TIP‑L)** , enables reciprocal acceptance of private pilot licences between EASA and the FAA.

The primary FAA guidance is **Advisory Circular 61‑143**, which describes the procedures for converting an EU Part‑FCL pilot licence to an FAA pilot certificate.

> **Effective Date:** The TIP‑L came into force on **18 May 2021** and applies to conversions under the BASA framework.

---

## BASA/TIP‑L vs. 14 CFR § 61.75 – Key Differences

| Feature | **BASA / TIP‑L Conversion** | **14 CFR § 61.75** |
|---------|----------------------------|-------------------|
| **Governing framework** | FAA‑EASA Bilateral Agreement | FAA regulation (foreign licence basis) |
| **Best for** | EASA Part‑FCL holders seeking formal FAA certificate conversion | Pilots needing FAA privileges tied to existing foreign licence |
| **Testing required** | May involve testing depending on case | No further testing |
| **Certificate status** | **Standalone** FAA certificate | **Foreign‑based** – limited by underlying foreign licence |
| **Primary reference** | AC 61‑143, TIP‑L document | 14 CFR Part 61, § 61.75 |

The key advantage of the **BASA/TIP‑L route** is that it results in a **standalone FAA certificate**, not a foreign‑based one tied to your EASA licence.

---

## Eligibility Requirements

To convert your EASA PPL under BASA/TIP‑L, you must:

| Requirement | Detail |
|-------------|--------|
| **Valid EASA Part‑FCL PPL** | Hold a current EASA PPL (or higher) with applicable ratings |
| **Valid medical certificate** | EASA medical **and** FAA medical certificate |
| **English proficiency** | Demonstrate ability to read, speak, write, and understand English |
| **FAA verification** | Complete the FAA verification process (Form AC 8060‑71) |
| **Ratings** | SEP, MEP, IR, and/or Night Rating may be converted |

> **Note:** The BASA/TIP‑L agreement applies specifically to **PPL(A)** licences and associated ratings (SEP, MEP, Night Rating). Higher licences (CPL, ATPL) require different procedures.

## Step‑by‑Step Conversion Process

### Step 1: Verify Your Licence (45–90 days)

Submit a verification request through the FAA's **IACRA** system or by mail using **FAA Form AC 8060‑71** (Verification of Authenticity of Foreign License and Medical Certification).

- **Via IACRA (Preferred):**
  1. Register at `iacra.faa.gov`
  2. Select **"START FOREIGN VERIFICATION PROCESS"**
  3. Upload your EASA PPL

- **By Mail:**
  - Complete FAA Form AC 8060‑71
  - Mail to: FAA Airmen Certification Branch AFB‑720, P.O. Box 25082, Oklahoma City, OK 73125

> The verification letter is valid for **6 months**.

### Step 2: Obtain an FAA Medical Certificate

You must hold a valid FAA medical certificate (at least Third‑Class) for the conversion. This is separate from your EASA medical.

### Step 3: Complete FAA Form 8710‑1

Complete the **FAA Form 8710‑1** (Airman Certificate and/or Rating Application). You can print this from IACRA.

### Step 4: Schedule In‑Person Appointment

You must appear in person before an FAA‑authorised official. You have **two options**:

**Option A: FAA Flight Standards District Office (FSDO) in the U.S.**
- Contact your selected FSDO upon receipt of the verification letter
- Allow **≥2 weeks** for appointment

**Option B: Designated Pilot Examiner – Foreign Pilot Examiner (DPE‑FPE) in Europe**
- Search the FAA Designee Locator (`designee.faa.gov`) for **DPE‑FPE**
- Some flight schools in Europe have contracted DPE‑FPEs on staff

### Step 5: Attend Appointment & Bring Required Documents

**Bring originals:**
- [ ] Verification of Authenticity Letter
- [ ] FAA Form 8710‑1
- [ ] EASA PPL (with all ratings)
- [ ] FAA medical certificate
- [ ] Proof of English proficiency
- [ ] Passport

### Step 6: Receive Your Certificate

- **Temporary certificate** issued on the spot
- **Permanent plastic card** mailed later by FAA

---

## What You Will Receive

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Certificate type** | U.S. Private Pilot Certificate (**Standalone**) |
| **Ratings** | Airplane Single‑Engine Land (ASEL) and other ratings from your EASA PPL |
| **Validity** | **Independent** of your EASA licence (not tied to it) |
| **Privileges** | Full private pilot privileges in U.S.‑registered (N‑number) aircraft |

---

## After Issuance – Maintaining Your FAA Certificate

| Requirement | Frequency |
|-------------|-----------|
| **Flight Review** | Every 24 calendar months (14 CFR § 61.56) |
| **Medical** | Must remain valid (FAA medical) |
| **Currency** | FAA recency requirements (14 CFR § 61.57) |
| **Ratings** | Instrument, night, and other ratings remain valid as per FAA rules |

---

## Important Notes

- **No written exam required** for PPL conversion under BASA/TIP‑L
- **A skill test may be required** depending on the specific case
- **Higher licences** (CPL, ATPL) are **not** covered by BASA/TIP‑L and require different procedures
- The **TIP‑L came into force on 18 May 2021** – conversions for licences issued after this date follow the TIP‑L framework

# Appendix A: ICAO Legal Framework for SPL to FAA Conversion

*Legal basis for EASA SPL → FAA Private Pilot Certificate under ICAO Annex 1 and 14 CFR § 61.75*

---

## ICAO Annex 1 – Personnel Licensing

**Chapter 2 – Licences and Ratings for Pilots**

**2.9 Glider pilot licence – Experience requirements**

> *"The applicant shall have completed not less than **15 hours of flight time** as a pilot of gliders including **two hours of solo flight time** during which not less than **40 launches and landings** have been performed."*

**Licence categories recognised:**

1. Private pilot (aeroplane, helicopter, powered-lift, airship)
2. Commercial pilot
3. Multi-crew pilot
4. Airline transport pilot
5. **Glider pilot** ← *Your SPL*
6. Free balloon pilot

---

## EASA SPL – ICAO Compliance

| ICAO Standard (Annex 1, 2.9) | EASA Part-SFCL | Status |
|-----------------------------|----------------|--------|
| 15 hours flight time | 15 hours (SFCL.130) | ✅ |
| 2 hours solo | 2 hours (SFCL.130) | ✅ |
| 40 launches/landings | 45 (SFCL.130) | ✅ exceeds |
| ICAO Class 2 medical | Part-MED Class 2 | ✅ |
| Private pilot level | SPL = private pilot level | ✅ |

> *"The European sailplane licence SPL meets **all ICAO requirements** for gliding."* — German Aviation Authority (DFS)

> *"The SPL complies with **all ICAO requirements** for gliding. With an ICAO Class 2 or ICAO Class 1 medical, the licence is valid **worldwide**."* — Belgian Gliding Training

---

## FAA 14 CFR § 61.75 – The Conversion

### § 61.75(a) – Eligibility

> *"A person who holds a **foreign pilot license at the private pilot level or higher** that was issued by a **contracting State to the Convention on International Civil Aviation** may apply for and be issued a **U.S. private pilot certificate** with the appropriate ratings."*

### § 61.75(b) – Issuance Requirements

> *"…**without any further showing of proficiency**, provided the applicant:*
>
> *(1) Holds a foreign pilot license **at the private pilot license level or higher**;*
>
> *(2) **Does not contain a limitation** stating the applicant has not met ICAO standards;*
>
> *(3) Does not hold a U.S. pilot certificate;*
>
> *(4) Holds a medical certificate **from the country that issued the foreign pilot license**;*
>
> *(5) Is able to read, speak, write, and understand English."*

### § 61.75(c) – Ratings Transfer

> *"Aircraft ratings listed on a person's foreign pilot license … may be placed on that person's U.S. pilot certificate."*

### § 61.75(e) – Operating Limitations

> *"A person who receives a U.S. private pilot certificate … is subject to the **limitations and restrictions on the person's U.S. certificate and foreign pilot license**."*

## FAA-EASA BASA / TIP‑L

### The Agreement

The **U.S.–EU Bilateral Aviation Safety Agreement (BASA)** , through **Annex 3 – Pilot Licensing**, enables reciprocal acceptance of private pilot licences between EASA and the FAA.

> *“The Pilot Licensing Annex (Annex 3) enables reciprocal acceptance of **private pilot licenses, as well as night and instrument ratings**, by both EASA and the FAA.”*

### AC 61-143 – Primary FAA Guidance

**FAA Advisory Circular 61-143** describes the procedures for converting an EU Part-FCL pilot license to an FAA pilot certificate.

> *“This AC describes how this conversion process is **different from** the process for obtaining an FAA pilot certificate issued on the basis of a foreign pilot license under 14 CFR § 61.75 **and for which the FAA certificate is limited by the foreign license**.”*

---

## 14 CFR § 61.75 – The Conversion

### § 61.75(a) – General

> *“A person who holds a current foreign pilot license issued by a contracting State to the Convention on International Civil Aviation may apply for and be issued a private pilot certificate with the appropriate ratings.”*

### § 61.75(b) – Issuance Requirements

> *“…may be issued a private pilot certificate based on the foreign pilot license **without any further showing of proficiency**, provided the applicant:*
>
> *(2) Holds a foreign pilot license that … **Does not contain an endorsement stating that the applicant has not met all of the standards of ICAO** for that license;*
>
> *(3) Does not currently hold a U.S. pilot certificate;*
>
> *(4) Holds a current medical certificate … issued by the country that issued the person's foreign pilot license;*
>
> *(5) Is able to read, speak, write, and understand the English language.”*

### § 61.75(c) – Aircraft Ratings

> *“Aircraft ratings listed on a person's foreign pilot license … may be placed on that person's U.S. pilot certificate.”*

### § 61.75(d) – Instrument Ratings

> *“A person who holds an instrument rating on the foreign pilot license may be issued an instrument rating … provided: (1) The person's foreign pilot license authorizes instrument privileges; (2) Within 24 months preceding application, the person passes the appropriate knowledge test.”*

### § 61.75(e) – Operating Limitations

> *“A person who receives a U.S. private pilot certificate … (3) Is subject to the **limitations and restrictions on the person's U.S. certificate and foreign pilot license**.”*

> *“Shall not exercise the privileges of that U.S. private pilot certificate when the person's foreign pilot license has been revoked or suspended.”*
